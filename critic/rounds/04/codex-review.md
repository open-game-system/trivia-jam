## Single largest gap

The TV is not being directed as a stage.

It behaves like a collection of responsive web panels placed on a large beige canvas. The question, guessing, reveal and finale should transform the whole room’s focus. Instead, `10b`, `10d`, `10e`, `10f` feel like separate layouts with inconsistent focal points. That weakens couch readability, dramatic timing and the shared-party feeling simultaneously.

## Five highest-impact fixes

1. **Create one persistent TV stage and enforce focal zones.**  
   Reserve stable areas for round status, question, playfield and player rail. Enlarge the ruler/options to occupy the central 60–70% in `10b`, `12b`, `13b`, and `14b`. Player names and guesses must remain readable from across a room, not merely at screenshot scale.

2. **Turn every answer reveal into a directed sequence.**  
   Use distinct beats: lock guesses → silence/anticipation → reveal the answer → move guesses toward it → identify winners → award points → update totals. Do not display everything simultaneously. This would fix the collisions in `10e` and `11e` and give the correct option priority in `13e`.

3. **Replace the reveal pile with lanes or winner cards.**  
   Each player needs a protected footprint containing avatar, name, guess and points. No overlapping badges or text. `11e-q2-reveal-settled-tv` should be legible with five or eight players, not merely three; the current composition already fails at three.

4. **Cut the typography system to three roles.**  
   Use one display face, one highly readable body face and one restrained metadata style. Remove most micro-labels and offset shadows from information-bearing text. Increase question, result and player-label sizes on TV; simplify the boxed density throughout the host screens.

5. **Rebuild the finale as the game’s largest event.**  
   In `20-game-over-tv` and `21-game-over-settled-tv`, reveal ranks one at a time, let the winner own the full screen, then present funny awards and a decisive rematch beat. Give each kid a personal ending—best guess, exact-answer count, comeback or rivalry—not only “2nd” and a point total.
